\section{Conclusion}

We presented TAMR-DTI, a multimodal DTI framework that represents drug-target
pairs through target-aware modulation and protein-side Mamba refinement. The
model addresses two limitations of fixed multimodal encoding: a ligand should
not be forced into the same conformer summary for every target, and long-range
protein refinement should not remove the explicit interaction modeling needed
for DTI prediction. TAMR-DTI therefore lets protein context reweight molecular
conformers, lets ligand context modulate protein tokens, and applies a gated
Mamba residual block only to the ordered protein sequence before BiIntention
cross-modal matching.

Across five repeated runs on four benchmark datasets, TAMR-DTI improves AUROC
and AUPRC on BindingDB, BioSNAP, Human, and C. elegans, with the
strongest improvement on BioSNAP. The Human benchmark still prevents a claim of
uniform superiority because its fixed-threshold scores remain weaker and more
variable than its ranking metrics. The BioSNAP ablation, conformer-count
comparison, and conformer-weight interpretability analysis nevertheless provide
consistent mechanism-level evidence: target-aware conformer aggregation
improves ranking quality and produces sample-specific conformer usage, while
the ablation results indicate that ligand-conditioned protein modulation and
protein-side Mamba refinement also contribute to the final prediction.

Overall, TAMR-DTI shows that pair-specific molecular geometry and protein-only
sequence refinement can be integrated without discarding explicit drug-target
matching. The resulting model is best viewed as a competitive, dataset-dependent
extension of a strong multimodal DTI backbone. Future work should strengthen the
evidence with matched multi-seed ablations, harder generalization splits, and
more direct structural supervision for conformer selection.

\subsubsection*{Data Availability}
The code for TAMR-DTI is publicly available at
\url{https://github.com/leleleocc/TAMR-DTI}. The benchmark datasets used in this
study are publicly available: BindingDB, BioSNAP from the Stanford SNAP
BioData collection, and the Human and C. elegans benchmarks introduced by Liu
et al. (2015), which are widely reused in prior DTI studies.
